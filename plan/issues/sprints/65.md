## Retrospective

**The defining lesson of s65: an architect-spec-first model produced WRONG
specs three times in a row — and deep-tracing senior-devs with per-process
binaryen verification are what actually shipped regression-free slices.**

**The headline win — the #1917 single-coercion-engine series completed.** The
four divergent coercion matrices were unified into one engine across Steps 1–3
(emitToString/Number/Boolean, #1960/#1962/#1963) + equality E3 (#1989), with
E6 (#1992) and the #2045 presence work (#1991) landing through the queue at
close. This is the architecture deliverable the s62 "Fable architecture sprint"
set out to bank, and it landed byte-neutral / regression-free — proof that the
trace-first, validate-per-process discipline scales to a broad-impact
shared-helper rewrite, not just narrow slices.

1. **Architect-spec-first mis-fired 3× on the substrate.** #2623-A (async box-
   depth), #2623-B (Promise-subclass identity), and #2580 M3 Stage A were each
   handed off with a spec that **mis-attributed the mechanism**. In all three a
   senior-dev had to deep-trace the actual failing path to find the real cause
   (single-box of an already-boxed capture; capability-ctor identity
   unification; inline-literal proto link) before a correct, regression-free
   slice could land. **Takeaway:** for value-rep/substrate work, route through a
   *verify-the-mechanism-first* trace, not a spec written from the issue
   narrative. A spec is a hypothesis until the binaryen output confirms it.

2. **Verify-first, per-process — NEVER in-process loops.** The in-process
   test262 loop falsely reports ~42 `compile_error reading 'kind'` (cross-test
   state bleed); the **per-process sharded runner is the only trustworthy
   signal**. Every s65 landing was validated per-process.

3. **The `merge_group` standalone floor (#2097) earned its keep.** It catches
   broad-impact regressions that PR-level checks miss (standalone floor runs
   only on `merge_group`). Every substrate slice this session was a broad-impact
   change; the floor is what made "0 regr" a measured claim, not a hope.

4. **The M3 framing was corrected mid-sprint.** The earlier "168-row functor
   lap" framing was wrong. The real lever is the **`Object.defineProperty`
   accessor cluster (181 of 266 files)** plus the **already-closed
   `__make_callback` leak**; the functor `.prototype=` lap is only **51 files**
   and is the **hardest / last** slice — escape-analysis-gated with a real
   #1888-floor-eject risk. Sequencing M3 by that corrected cluster composition
   (accessors first, functor-lap last) is the s66 plan, not a row-count lap.

**Process keepers for s66:**
- Substrate epics → trace-first, spec-as-hypothesis; senior-dev (Opus) owns the
  trace, architect documents the *confirmed* mechanism.
- All broad-impact validation through `merge_group` floor + per-process runner.
- One-shot enqueue, never re-enqueue (no merge-queue churn this session).
